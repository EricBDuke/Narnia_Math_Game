Notes and answers for “Style and taste”
5.Money

This is the one people feel awkward about. Say it kindly and plainly.

How to bring it up (module p26–27): Tell her why it matters first: “I want you to love this and not feel stretched. If I know a real number, I won't design something you can't afford.” Real design takes time and money; TV makeovers aren't real life. Ask her to be honest, because a vague budget wastes both your time.

Bring back to Tucker

Right after the meeting, tap Copy all my answers and paste it to me. Also send:

  • Her answers in every section, in her own words where you can
  • The budget number, what it includes, and her top spending priorities
  • The finish date and any key dates
  • Room size, ceiling height, and every door and window with its size and spot (a photo of your sketch is fine)
  • Photos of each wall, close-ups, and the walkthrough video
  • Her inspiration pictures, plus what she said she liked and didn't like in each
  • Her must-keep pieces, and the fees you agreed (mood board and project)

I'll lay out your next steps in order.

What happens next

The module's process, in order. This meeting is the consultation in Phase 1. See every step →

  1. Phase 1 · Discover: discovery call → follow-up email → this meeting → second follow-up email with your notes and a mood board offer (module p64–71)
  2. Phase 2 · Plan: mood board → quote → show her the mood board (2nd meeting) → design brief → letter of acceptance signed → full room check and measurements → design development → design presentation → sample board and cost schedule (module p72–77, p86)
  3. Phase 3 · Implement: order and buy, any trade work, install, style, reveal, final walk-through, wrap up, and a follow-up a few months later (module p78–82)
