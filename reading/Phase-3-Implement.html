PHASE 3 - IMPLEMENT

CONSTRUCTION

Once you have the final design made up and the client has approved it, you can then move onto the construction phase (if construction is required) and schedule in your trades. Note that this part is not always managed by the interior designer. Often the plans/concepts are handed over to the client for implementation by themselves or the contractor. If you’re uncomfortable managing trades, remember it’s not a mandatory part of the job.

The work will be scheduled in accordance with your clients schedule, your project schedule, and your trades’ availability. When you are meeting with the trades, be sure to ask the following questions:

  • Who is responsible for the purchase of materials?
  • What is their lead time from quotation acceptance to starting the project?
  • How long the task will take?
  • Do they require a deposit and if so, how much?
  • What is their pricing structure? Ask them to itemize the materials separately to labour.
  • Explain the proposed schedule of work. What is the optimum time of the project for them to perform the tasks?
  • Provide an entire overview of the project. Do they see any potential issues, hold ups or problems with your schedule? Seek their advice on how to rectify or avoid any issues, remembering that they are always the expert, and it is better that you work with the tradespeople not against them.

A NOTICE ABOUT SAFETY FOR CONSTRUCTION SITES

Visiting a construction site to meet with a client or meet with them at their home whilst work is being carried out, can be dangerous. For these situations, your safety and the safety of those around you are paramount, and there are several rules that will need to be followed whilst on site.

Be aware of your surroundings, and check the area around you for hazards or things on the ground that you can trip on. Make eye contact with equipment operators. If a safety path has been marked on the ground, follow it. If you need to step outside of this, contact a site supervisor for help.

PUBLIC LIABILITY

In the world of self-employment, it is common practice for contractors to hire subcontractors to get a job done. This allows contractors to focus on managing projects and clients while “subbing out” more specialised tasks.

If a subcontractor makes a mistake on a jobsite and doesn’t have insurance, both the contractor and subcontractor’s businesses could be held liable. Clients should be paying subcontractors directly unless you are also a licensed contractor. Check your states rules and regulations and consider what insurance is appropriate for you or seek independent insurance advice.

GENERAL CLOTHING

  • If you are visiting a construction site, your clothing attire should be practical enough to withstand walking on uneven ground, going up and down ladders or climbing over obstacles.
  • Wear fully closed shoes.
  • Be prepared for any weather event, such as rain, cold or the heat of summer.

PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE)

You will be required to wear some form of PPE when you visit a building site. If the scale of the construction work being undertaken on site is minimal, you may only be required to wear closed shoes, but if the work is more complex, the amount of protection required will be increased.

The list of PPE for the actual trades and services working on the building site is more complex, but as a visitor, there are some pieces you may be required to wear:

Boots – Any time you visit a building site, you are to wear fully closed shoes. If you are intending on walking around the active building areas, you should invest in a pair of work boots to protect your feet from items falling on them.

Ear plugs – These are to be used in building sites that are operating machinery. They are more commonly worn by the machine operators, but in some cases, you may be asked to wear them.

Goggles – These are used to protect your eyes from dust or particles. This form of PPE is more commonly used for the machinery operators, but if you are entering an area with high dust or particles, you may be asked to wear them.

Hard hats – These are to protect your head from any falling items. If you are entering a construction site with any work being performed above shoulder height, you will be required to wear a hard hat.

High visibility clothing – More commonly called “High Vis” clothing, these are brightly colored fluorescent yellow, orange or pink clothes worn by everyone visiting or working on the construction site. As a visitor, you will be asked to wear a vest that is worn over your clothes.

PURCHASING MERCHANDISE

You may be required to source all the materials, furniture, accessories and inclusions required for the project if the client has hired you to do so. This can be a daunting task, especially for a large scale project, but this can be made easier by breaking down the project down into smaller, more manageable sections, such as by like individual rooms.

The easiest way to keep your clients happy is with complete transparency. Ensure they are aware of any warehousing, delivery fees, and the initial shipping fee. Also, ensure you use a licensed, reputable warehouse company, as they will make sure the items are not damaged and are stored safely.

Once ready, place any orders for furnishings and decor with your vendors and suppliers. Be sure to follow up regularly with your vendors to keep the project on track, receive and inspect goods, and catch deficiencies early where possible. All while keeping your client abreast of progress and new developments.

IMPLEMENTATION

Be sure to collect payments from any outstanding invoices before the installation and styling phase. Installation is generally scheduled 12-14 weeks after order placement and lasts 1-3 days depending on your project’s size.

Be sure to schedule the installations of any custom millwork and closets, electronics, wallpaper and window coverings. This is all done prior to the delivery of furniture and accessories, so your goods stay safely out of harm’s way.

STYLING

Once the moment has come for the final deliveries to arrive, ask your client to vacate the premises while you work your magic, installing furniture, creating the room layout, and styling.

REVEAL

Once the project is ready to be presented, let the client know that they can come home for a project reveal. This is an exciting stage of the design process! If possible, and with your clients permission, ask if you can film the reveal to share on your social platforms. You can also either schedule to have the space photographed professionally before you reveal it to your client or after depending on what suits your client.

PROJECT COMPLETE

Once you have done a walk through with your client, compile a list of any outstanding items, and resolve these items as efficiently as possible. You’ll then apply your retainer to any final invoices and present your client with a balance invoice or refund where applicable. Be sure to thank your client for their patience and perseverance during the often long but oh-so-rewarding project process.

FOLLOW-UP EMAIL

After a few months, you can follow up with your client to see how they are liking their new space/s. Here, you can also send out a survey or ask for a testimonial.

A NOTICE ABOUT WORKING IN THE FIELD

What we have put together in this module is a professional approach to working with your clients and will be helpful when starting out.

If you are working for yourself, you can use the templates and formats we have created, keeping in mind each project will be different, so you will need to adapt them to suit each job.

If you are working with an established interior design firm, they will have their own templates and formats to work with.
