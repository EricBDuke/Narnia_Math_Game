There must be someone you know who could do with the help of an interior designer. You are to find someone to act as a client and select a room in their house that needs redesigning.

For this project, you cannot act as the client yourself, but you can ask a family member or friend to play the client’s part.

The design and decoration of the room can be real or fictional, but the actual room it is based on will need to be real as you will complete the full design process (real or fictional) from start to end.

The room must be one dry room from the following list:

  • Living
  • Media
  • Dining
  • Nursery
  • Study
  • Bedroom

The project will involve a complete redesign of the room, including flooring, paint, lighting, furniture, fixed electrical equipment, cabinetry, walls, window furnishings, artwork and accessories as dictated by the room type.

Treat this exercise as a real design project by following the steps below:

The discovery call, whether by phone, email or video, is your first contact with a potential client. After the call, confirm via email what was discussed during the phone call and ask the client to source images to describe their design style when you meet either in person or virtually.

NOTE: The email or details of the discovery call are not part of this assignment.

STEP 1 - FIRST CLIENT MEETING

Create a template for the first meeting with your client and prepare a series of questions you would ask. You can refer to the ‘Questions to ask during the consultation’ section in the module for inspiration on which questions to include.

To create your template, you can use this Client Engagement Template or use Word, Canva, PowerPoint etc.

Be as creative as you want, but cover what you need to go away and develop a suggested scheme. In the meeting, be sure to obtain the client’s budget, the wish list of items to include and the time frame for the project.

Discuss your fee to create a mood board and your fee for the project using one of the fee types outlined in the module. Make sure to record the two fees and the budget in the meeting report.

STEP 2 - AFTER THE CONSULTATION

Email your client the client engagement form and the invoice for the mood board. When the invoice has been paid, the client is only engaging you to develop a concept, and they have not engaged your services for the entire project at this stage.

NOTE: In the real world, you would send the client a separate invoice for the mood board, but this is not required for this assignment.

STEP 3 - MOOD BOARD DESIGN CONCEPT

Put together a mood board using images inspired by your interpretation of the client’s brief. Creating a visually appealing mood board is a wonderful opportunity to put your creative skills learned in Module 3 into actionable use. Remember, a mood board is more than just a collection of images, it’s a visual tool that communicates your ideas and sparks inspiration. It’s an amalgamation of different elements that tell a cohesive story. When creating your boards, you might like to refer back to the ‘How to Visually Communicate Ideas to Your Client’ chapter from Module 3.

Think about how you can create a balance of inspiration, colors, textures, and shapes on your board. Highlight key elements and create variety through different types of images. Remember that creating a mood board should be a fun and creative process. Don’t limit yourself – experiment with different layouts, combine unexpected elements, and play with colors and fonts. This is your chance to express yourself creatively and bring your ideas to life.

STEP 4 – SECOND CLIENT MEETING

Present the mood board to your client, noting feedback and any suggested changes.

STEP 5 – LETTER OF ACCEPTANCE

When the client is happy with the concept and has confirmed you can proceed with the project, you create a letter of acceptance. The letter should include the following:

  • Break down of your fee structure
  • Details of your fee payment schedule
  • Budget breakdown for the furniture, art and accessories only
  • State in the letter a formal contract agreement is attached

Remember that your letter should only include the services and items you are providing, not the contractor, trades, specialty services and pricing for custom items.

Note that when you are working as an interior designer, you would engage the services of a lawyer to create a standard interior design contract agreement that you complete for each project. This is not required for this assignment, only the contract cover letter is.

STEP 6 – SITE EVALUATION

With the contract signed, you can now conduct a site evaluation. First, download and complete this Site Evaluation Template. You can use this template or create your own.

The site evaluation is to include photographs of the room and a hand drawn floor plan using the measurements taken on-site. Make sure to be 100% accurate with the positioning of the windows and doors.

STEP 7 – SAMPLE BOARD AND COSTING SCHEDULE

Now that you have gathered all the information, it’s time to source the products and create a sample board to showcase the final design.

All selections are to be included on the board and numbered with a legend. Along with the board, create a schedule of the cost of the items as seen in the example in the module.

The schedule will need to show the total price for the project selections, and you tutor will need to see that you have met the client budget as outlined on your client engagement form.

LETTER OF ACCEPTANCE

OME

1 Ridge Rd, Upper Plenty VIC 3756 Australia janehomes@ome.com 0400900100

03/02/2023

Dear Mr and Mrs Sunday,

Thank you for choosing ‘OME to undertake the design work on your property located at 1 Lovers Lane Gin Gin, QLD, Australia.

For this project, ‘OME will carry out the following services:

To convert the existing multipurpose room into a living room. The scope of works includes the design, installation of a gas fireplace, construction of fireplace surround, product selection (sample board), product specification (including new flooring, new ceiling, window furnishings, lighting (fixtures, finishes and equipment schedule), product order/purchase, project management and styling services.

The Carpenter, Plasterer, Gas Plumber, Electrician and Painter to be involved in the project are to be employed by the client.

The selection of trades to undertake works on the building is to be made by ‘OME.

The project management of the works carried out on site is to be carried out by ‘OME.

Mr and Mrs Sunday will pay the trades and services employed to carry out the work at 1 Lovers Lane Gin Gin directly, and the purchase and payment of all goods and materials is the responsibility of the client.

The services supplied by ‘OME will be at 15% of the total budget ($5,200)

‘OME will invoice the fees ($5,200) at three stages over the course of the work.

Those stages will be as follows:

50% ($2,600) deposit required at the start of the design work

25% ($1,300) progress payment at the 4-week mark

The balance to be paid upon completion of the project
